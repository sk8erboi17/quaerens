\maketitle
This note explains what the test data in \texttt{false\_nth\_derivatives.tex} is used for. That file is input for the engine in \texttt{main.c}; it is read by the test \texttt{test\_derivatives.c} in this folder.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Purpose.} The file holds 44 false statements on derivatives. It is an anti-regression test: the engine must never report any of them as proved.

\item \textbf{The kinds of error.} A wrong phase, sign, factorial, exponent or power of the constant (items 1 to 5, 8 to 16); a formula that is right only for $n \le 3$ or $n \le 5$ (items 6 and 7); a function that depends on $n$ (item 17); a wrong derivative of fixed order (items 18 to 27). Items 28 to 44 are the true items 31 to 45 of \texttt{nth\_derivatives.tex} with one thing changed: a term or an exponent of a sum, the upper limit of a product, a sign, a missing $\ln 2$ or a power of it too many, a lower base case ($n \ge 3$ instead of $n \ge 4$), $-\frac{n\pi}{2}$ for $+\frac{n\pi}{2}$, a missing term; and two formulas that are right up to $n = 4$ and $n = 2$ and wrong after.

\item \textbf{How falsity was checked.} By the independent oracle of \texttt{tools/fuzz/fuzz\_derivatives.py} (mode \texttt{check}), which finds every item false. Item 17 too: the oracle computes the derivative of $x^n$ for each $n$ separately, and $n!$ is not $n!\,x$.

\item \textbf{Items 18, 19 and 20.} Before chapter 27 the program read the Leibniz sign $\frac{d}{dx}$ as a fraction of the letters $d$ and $x$, and reported $\frac{d}{dx} 5 = \frac{5}{x}$ as proved, with a verified certificate: as algebra it is $\frac{d}{dx} \cdot 5 = \frac{5}{x}$. Read as a derivative it is false. These items keep that reading from coming back.

\item \textbf{Expected result.} The line \texttt{PASS: 44 of 44 false statements rejected}. Most are answered \texttt{FALSO}, with the order $n$ or the point $x$ where a derivative computed directly differs from the formula; the engine says \texttt{FALSO} only where that derivative agrees with the one read from the Taylor series of the function (rule 27i). Items 6, 7 and 43 are \texttt{NON DIMOSTRATO}: they are right for every order the engine compares one by one, and only the inductive step stops them.

\item \textbf{How to run it.} From the top folder of the repository:
\begin{verbatim}
cc -std=c11 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -o /tmp/test_derivatives \
    tests/derivatives/test_derivatives.c && /tmp/test_derivatives
\end{verbatim}
\end{enumerate}
